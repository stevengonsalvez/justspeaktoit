## Design Context

### Users
Just Speak to It users are people who want to capture thoughts, messages, notes, and voice-driven workflows quickly without managing a complex recording tool. They may be moving between apps, dictating under time pressure, or using OpenClaw hands-free. The job is to turn speech into usable text with confidence, privacy clarity, and minimal friction.

### Brand Personality
Calm, precise, capable. The interface should feel like a polished voice cockpit: immediate enough for capture, quiet enough for repeated daily use, and premium enough to trust with private speech and API keys.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use a rich but restrained light interface with layered surfaces, deep teal as the primary action color, ink text, muted lavender/slate support surfaces, and warm amber accents for model/status details. Avoid generic white screens, oversized empty space, plain form demos, cartoonish voice UI, and one-note teal-only palettes.

### Design Principles
1. Make the primary action unmistakable: recording must be visually central, tactile, and stateful.
2. Show operational confidence: provider, privacy, sync, and processing state should be visible through compact status chips.
3. Use depth with discipline: layered panels and subtle gradients should frame workflows without hiding content in decorative cards.
4. Preserve fast repeated use: key actions remain thumb-accessible, labels are direct, and transcript text gets the highest reading priority.
5. Validate visually on device: every major UI change should be checked through an emulator screenshot, not only build output.
